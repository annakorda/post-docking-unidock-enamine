References -- rationale for the Asp116 salt-bridge distance criterion used
by interaction_filter_fast.py (charged N to Asp116 CG carbon, 5.0A cutoff)

1. Barlow, D.J.; Thornton, J.M. "Ion-pairs in proteins."
   Journal of Molecular Biology, 1983, 168(4), 867-885.
   DOI: 10.1016/S0022-2836(83)80079-5
   PMID: 6887253
   -- Seminal reference establishing the <=4A distance criterion for ion
   pairs in proteins, derived from 38 real protein structures. Same paper
   LUNA's own Ionic interaction default (max_dist_attract_inter, 6A) cites.
   Our own cutoff (5.0A) is intentionally more permissive than this paper's
   4A, matching this project's prior analyze_asp_contact.py value, not a
   direct reproduction of Barlow & Thornton's own number.

2. Shi, L.; Javitch, J.A. "The Binding Site of Aminergic G Protein-Coupled
   Receptors: The Transmembrane Segments and Second Extracellular Loop."
   Annual Review of Pharmacology and Toxicology, 2002, 42, 437-467.
   DOI: 10.1146/annurev.pharmtox.42.091101.144224
   -- Directly relevant to this target class: describes the salt bridge
   between a biogenic amine ligand's protonated amine and the conserved
   Asp (Asp3.32 in standard GPCR numbering, Asp116 in this receptor's own
   numbering) as essential and conserved across every aminergic GPCR
   identified, canonical distance under 4A.

3. "Salt Bridges in Proteins: The Invisible Anchors."
   sdrfoundation.org (general web article, not a peer-reviewed journal --
   no DOI). https://sdrfoundation.org/salt-bridges-protein-stability
   -- Corroborating general-audience source, confirms measuring salt-bridge
   distance from the carboxylate CARBON (not the oxygens) to the amine
   nitrogen is an established, real convention, matching what we measure
   to (Asp116's CG) rather than an OD1/OD2-based point.
